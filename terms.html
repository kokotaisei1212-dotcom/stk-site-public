2. Agreement

Taking an STK key with POST /v1/keys means you accept these terms and the Data Processing Agreement. If you take a key for an organisation, you confirm you may bind it.

3. The service

An API at api.stk-evidence.com, and two files, stk.py and stk.mjs, that run in your code. Before your AI hands a conversation to a person, the file decides finish or escalate on your side from one set of rules, the same for every company, published in full and signed by STK at The rules in full and GET /v1/rules. Every change to the rules is dated in GET /v1/rules?history=1. On finish it sends STK the seal of your row (SHA-256 of 32 random bytes and the row), and STK signs it with the time and the rules version: that is a permission. The row and the random bytes stay with you (The ledger). With the seal STK receives the time your file recorded the action, and for a seal sent from the file's queue the grant STK signed; nothing else: a request with any other field is refused. A report (POST /v1/incident) carries the intent you report and, if you give them, its jurisdictions.

The files stk.py, stk.mjs and stk_verify.py are released under the MIT License, Copyright (c) 2026 Taisei Izawa (井澤大成). The rules are under CC BY 4.0. The API and the service are under these Terms.

4. Keys

POST /v1/keys with your email answers with your test key and a page of Stripe's where you register a US bank account, once: you sign in to your bank, and authorize STK to debit that account for what STK bills you under these Terms. There is no live key before it; when it is registered, your live key is given and works at once. Nothing else is ever asked of you. STK keeps only the keys' hashes, never sees the account's full number, and keeps your address itself, in your Stripe customer's metadata, not as Stripe's customer email: Stripe sends you nothing of its own, except the one email the US bank network's rules require when you authorize the debits. STK sends you one email a month (from 2026-12-11 12:00 UTC), and one other only: a notice to the account's address when new keys are asked for it without a key and with a bank account other than its own (Docs). Keep the keys confidential; you are responsible for use under them. With its live key, an account may take more pairs of keys, each with a use it declares; the use changes no price, and no key stops. The same address takes new keys of its first use only with its live key.

Every key of your account stops at once when its live key is sent to POST /v1/keys/revoke (a test key stops nothing); the account, its bank account and its invoices stay. Nothing signed after that is billed; what was signed before is billed as usual. New keys come from the account's live key, from the account's own bank account proven on a bank page, or from another bank account 7 days after they are asked unless the live key cancels them; each is in GET /v1/ledger (Docs).

Your row stays with you, in the shape you choose: intent and trigger are all it must have. Of a row STK receives only its seal, 32 bytes from which nothing can be read back, and the time your file recorded it, so what you put in the row never reaches STK.

5. What STK permits

For an account whose debit was refused for good, STK signs nothing (section 7, a condition of these Terms). The rules say escalate whenever the trigger is not unknown, for other, for the intents never permitted, for any intent not on their lists, and for an intent STK closed (closed names); clause by clause in The rules in full. Otherwise they say finish. There is no setting per company. STK never writes to your systems; your code acts on the answer.

6. Reports

POST /v1/incident with the receipt and signature of one permission of your own live key, and the intent it was for, leaves one dated report. Every report is public in GET /v1/rules?history=1, with its intent and date, never the company. A report changes no rule and no answer, for you or anyone. Only STK closes an intent (closed names), in a new version of the rules, dated in the history. An intent is Never only with a law that names a person for it, written beside it with the address of its official text. The line was read on 2026-11-25, from the laws named in the record, and it does not change: no later version takes an intent off the Never list or lets an AI finish what it sends to a person. A new version can add a trigger, never take one off or change its words. STK's server does not start with a version that breaks Lock 1 (the law) or Lock 2 (the triggers): the locks.

7. Fees

In short: $5 per action an AI finishes alone. $0 when a person must decide. The rest of this section is what that means exactly.

One price for every key (Price), whatever the action or the jurisdiction. A key may be taken with a use you declare (POST /v1/keys with use): it is kept as information and changes no price.

Under the rules for conversations (the domain conversation, what it covers), $5 for each permission: each seal your live key sent to POST /v1/ask that STK signed (the same seal twice the same day is one). For each day (UTC), the permissions of that day less the calls of that day to POST /v1/joined (a call with the seal of a permission of the day before, until 00:10 UTC, counts for that day, and for both days when STK's server started again after the day before began), never below 0: an answer of STK that came too late to be used is not billed (STK took more than 3 seconds while no grant of your account held, or your code stopped waiting and the file told STK); under a grant the file finished, and that permission is billed; a person who joins the same day (UTC) takes that permission off the bill; one who joins on a later day does not, and the permission stays billed. stk.py and stk.mjs call STK only when the rules say finish, and do not call again for a conversation and intent that got finish the same day. Calls with your test key are signed the same, marked which_key: test, and are never billed. A seal STK did not sign is not billed. On a day (UTC) STK's server started again, the permissions are the larger of the counts before and after the start, never their sum, and what was not yet written to Stripe when the server stopped is not billed; a day whose signing key STK made invalid is billed nothing, credited with a Stripe credit note if it was already invoiced: the bill can come out lower than the number of actions STK permitted. It cannot come out higher: a seal sent again after a restart is counted in the count after the start, and the day is billed the larger of the two counts, not their sum. STK's own libraries do not send the same seal twice. STK does not see your row, so it bills a signed seal whatever the row says. The price is the same for every company; nothing you send changes it.

STK is paid one way only: from the US bank account you registered, debited by STK (ACH). There is no other way, for any company in any country. Each month (UTC) is closed on the 1st of the next, from 01:00 UTC, and its amount is debited that day: $5 for each permission billed, as above. A month above $999,999.99 is debited as several debits, as few and as equal as can be (the last can be a few cents less), together its amount to the cent; each is its own invoice in Stripe, and the month's email gives one amount. A debit counts as paid for signing from when it begins, before your bank answers. If your bank refuses a debit for good (the account closed, no such account, an invalid account number, the account restricted, or debits not authorized), STK signs no seal of your account (a condition of these Terms, not of the rules), so stk.py and stk.mjs answer escalate, until it is paid: registering another bank account (POST /v1/keys/bank) debits it at once and signs again from then. If your bank refuses it for any other reason (want of money among them), STK debits it again 3 days later, at most 3 times in all; STK signs as usual throughout, and if the third try is refused, STK does not try again and that month is not owed. What STK signed while a debit was under way is billed as any day. Nothing is billed while STK signs nothing. GET /v1/health with your key says whether your account is in that state. If STK changes the day of the month it debits, it says so here 7 days before. Each billed permission is a signed receipt you kept; GET /v1/ledger gives each day's counts.

An account has no limit on what it signs or owes. STK stops signing for it only when its bank refuses a debit for good.

STK makes no refund on request. What you are billed is set by the number of permissions STK signed, and you can check each one, line by line.

A debit of any account disputed with its bank (the bank takes it back at your request): STK keeps signing. A dispute decided against STK: STK debits the account once more for the amount the bank took back; if the bank refuses that debit for good, STK stops as for any debit refused for good. Fees for closed months remain payable.

What you send is not shown to other companies, not sold, and not used to rank or score anyone. One thing is shown to every company: a report you make with POST /v1/incident, as its intent and date, never who made it.

10. Your AI and your customers

STK returns an answer and never writes to your systems; your AI and your staff act on it. You remain responsible for what is said to your customers. Using STK does not mean you comply with any law; each company remains responsible for its own legal duties.

11. Availability

The service has no uptime commitment. When STK does not answer and no grant holds, stk.py and stk.mjs answer escalate, and your code must hand the conversation to a person, as it would without STK.

12. Leaving and ending

To leave, send one call with your live key to POST /v1/stop. Nothing is asked and nothing else is needed: every key stops at once, stk.py and stk.mjs hand every conversation to a person, what is owed (the days so far and any invoice not paid) is debited once, and then STK ends its authorization to debit your bank account. Nothing after it is billed. From then the address can take keys again, as a new account. Not calling STK also bills nothing: no month with no permission is billed. STK receives and keeps no row. Each day that is billed something is an invoice item with Stripe, holding that day's two counts (permissions, people joining) and what was billed; with the invoices, Stripe keeps them as payment records, and STK does not delete them. Each report is deleted 7 years after it was made. Your account (the address and the hashed keys, with Stripe) is deleted 7 years after its last day with an ask or a person joining, or 30 days after it was made if its live key was never used: an account in use stays. Stripe's invoices name the account by its id, not by its address. GET /v1/ledger gives your days' counts at any time. STK may end these terms with a notice in the Changelog. Fees for closed months remain payable.

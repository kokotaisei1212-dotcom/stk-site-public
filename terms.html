2. Agreement

Taking an STK key with POST /v1/keys means you accept these terms and the Data Processing Agreement. If you take a key for an organisation, you confirm you may bind it.

3. The service

An API at api.stk-evidence.com, and two files, stk.py and stk.mjs, that run in your code. Before your AI hands a conversation to a person, the file decides finish or escalate on your side from one set of rules, the same for every company, published in full and signed by STK at Rules and GET /v1/rules. Every change to the rules is dated in GET /v1/rules?history=1. On finish it sends STK the seal of your row (SHA-256 of 32 random bytes and the row), and STK signs it with the time and the rules version: that is a permission. The row and the random bytes stay with you (The ledger). With the seal STK receives the time your file recorded the action, and for a seal sent from the file's queue the grant STK signed; nothing else: a request with any other field is refused.

4. Keys

POST /v1/keys with your email answers with your test key and your live key, once, and a page of Stripe's where you register a card; the live key signs only once the card is registered. STK keeps only the keys' hashes, never sees the card's number, and sends one email only: from 2026-12-05 12:00 UTC, a notice to the account's address when new keys are asked for it without a key and with a card other than its own (Docs). Keep them confidential; you are responsible for use under them. The same address takes new keys only with its live key, and the old ones then stop at once; or, if its keys were never used, again 10 minutes after they were made.

Your keys stop at once when either is sent to POST /v1/keys/revoke; the account, its card and its invoices stay. Nothing signed after that is billed; what was signed before is billed as usual. New keys come from the account's live key, from the account's own card proven on a card page, or from another card 7 days after they are asked unless the live key cancels them; each is in GET /v1/ledger (Docs).

Your row stays with you, in the shape you choose: intent and trigger are all it must have. Of a row STK receives only its seal, 32 bytes from which nothing can be read back, and the time your file recorded it, so what you put in the row never reaches STK.

5. What STK permits

The rules say escalate for an account with an invoice unpaid 60 days after its due date, whenever the trigger is not unknown, for other, for the intents never permitted, for any intent not on their lists, and for an intent STK closed (closed names); clause by clause in The rules. Otherwise they say finish. There is no setting per company. STK never writes to your systems; your code acts on the answer.

6. Reports

POST /v1/incident with the receipt and signature of one permission of your own live key, and the intent it was for, leaves one dated report. Every report is public in GET /v1/rules?history=1, with its intent and date, never the company. A report changes no rule and no answer, for you or anyone. Only STK closes an intent (closed names), in a new version of the rules, dated in the history. An intent is Never only with a law that names a person for it, written beside it with the address of its official text; when that law stops naming a person, the intent is permitted; every intent added to the Permitted list is published with its reasons; a new version can add a trigger, never take one off or change its words. STK's server does not start with a version that breaks Lock 1 (the law) or Lock 2 (the triggers): the locks.

7. Fees

Under the rules for conversations (the domain conversation, what it covers), $5 for each permission: each seal your live key sent to POST /v1/ask that STK signed (the same seal twice the same day is one). For each day (UTC), the permissions of that day less the calls of that day to POST /v1/joined (a call with the seal of a permission of the day before, until 00:10 UTC, counts for that day, and for both days when STK's server started again after the day before began), never below 0: a person who joins the same day (UTC) takes that permission off the bill; one who joins on a later day does not, and the permission stays billed. stk.py and stk.mjs call STK only when the rules say finish, and do not call again for a conversation and intent that got finish the same day. Calls with your test key are signed the same, marked which_key: test, and are never billed. A seal STK did not sign is not billed. On a day (UTC) STK's server started again, the permissions are the larger of the counts before and after the start, never their sum, and what was not yet written to Stripe when the server stopped is not billed; a day whose signing key STK made invalid is billed nothing, credited with a Stripe credit note if it was already invoiced: the bill can come out lower than the number of actions STK permitted. It cannot come out higher: a seal sent again after a restart is counted in the count after the start, and the day is billed the larger of the two counts, not their sum. STK's own libraries do not send the same seal twice. STK does not see your row, so it bills a signed seal whatever the row says. The price is the same for every company; nothing you send changes it.

Each week (Monday to Sunday, UTC) is closed the next Monday. Stripe makes its invoice, due when issued, and charges it that day to the card registered with the live key; Stripe keeps the card, STK never sees its number. If the card is declined, Stripe tries again, and the invoice stays open until paid. An invoice still unpaid 60 days after it was issued: STK signs no seal of your account (a condition of these Terms, not of the rules), so stk.py and stk.mjs answer escalate, until it is paid; nothing is billed meanwhile. GET /v1/health with your key says whether your account is in that state. Each billed permission is a signed receipt you kept; GET /v1/ledger gives each day's counts and bill. Fees for closed weeks remain payable.

8. What reaches other companies

What you send is not shown to other companies, not sold, and not used to rank or score anyone. One thing is shown to every company: a report you make with POST /v1/incident, as its intent and date, never who made it.

9. Acceptable use

Use the service only for conversations you are entitled to process. Do not try to extract other companies' data, resell the service, or use it for decisions the law reserves for people. Do not use STK's output (its permissions, its rules or your ledger) to build, train or improve a product or model that competes with STK.

10. Your AI and your customers

STK returns an answer and never writes to your systems; your AI and your staff act on it. You remain responsible for what is said to your customers. Using STK does not mean you comply with any law; each company remains responsible for its own legal duties.

11. Availability

The service has no uptime commitment. When STK does not answer and no grant holds, your code must do what it was about to do with a conversation.

12. Leaving and ending

To leave, stop calling STK. Nothing is billed for weeks with no permission. STK receives and keeps no row. Each day that is billed something is an invoice item with Stripe, holding that day's two counts (permissions, people joining) and what was billed; with the invoices, Stripe keeps them as payment records, and STK does not delete them. Each report is deleted 7 years after it was made. Your account (the address and the hashed keys, with Stripe) is deleted 7 years after its last day with an ask or a person joining, or 30 days after it was made if its live key was never used: an account in use stays. The address stays on the invoices Stripe keeps. GET /v1/ledger gives your days' counts at any time. We may end these terms with a notice in the Changelog. Fees for closed weeks remain payable.

14. Changes

We post changes on this page with a new date. A new version of the rules is named by its number and the day it was published, its text fixed by its SHA-256, and dated in GET /v1/rules?history=1. An addition to the rules (an intent permitted, a trigger, a clause, a reason or an explanation added, an error corrected, a new domain) is published the day it is made.

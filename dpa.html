DPA

Data Processing Agreement

Version of November 27, 2026

This agreement is the same for every company and is not negotiated.

1. Parties and scope

Controller: the person or organisation that took the STK key ("you").
Processor: Taisei Izawa (井澤大成), Toyama, Japan ("we").

Contact: izatai12@icloud.com. A company that needs a signed copy with the full registered address can ask at the same address.

This agreement forms part of the Terms of Service and applies from the moment a key is taken until your account is deleted. Where it conflicts with the Terms on personal data, this agreement prevails.

2. The processing

PurposeTo sign, for each permission your code decides from the published rules, its seal (SHA-256 of 32 random bytes and your row, both kept by you) with the time and the rules version; to keep each day's counts; and to count the permissions, for the bill.
Data subjectsThe customers who contact you, only through what your code puts in its rows; STK receives no row, only its seal.
Personal dataOf the customers who contact you: none is asked for. No text, no audio, no customer id: a request carries the seal and the time the library recorded the action (and, for a seal from the library's queue, the grant STK signed); with any other field it is refused. A report the Company chooses to make (POST /v1/incident) carries the intent reported and, if given, its jurisdictions. Of the Company: the email address of its account, which STK keeps to send the month's email and the notices in the Terms, and the hashes of its keys (Privacy).
Special categoriesNot intended. Do not put health or other special-category data in any field.
DurationRows of asks: none kept by STK. Each billed day's counts and each invoice: kept by Stripe as payment records. Each report: deleted 7 years after it was made. The account: deleted 7 years after its last day with an ask or a person joining (30 days after it was made, if its live key was never used).

3. Your instructions

We process the data only on your documented instructions: your API calls. If we think an instruction breaks the law we tell you.

4. Confidentiality

Only the processor has access to the systems and the data. The sub-processors in section 6 hold it only to run the service. Nobody else is given access.

5. Security

  • TLS on every request.
  • Keys kept only as a keyed hash (HMAC-SHA256).
  • Each account sees only its own days' counts, by its key.

6. Sub-processors

You authorise the sub-processors listed on Subprocessors. Before adding or replacing one we update that list with a new date at least 14 days ahead. If you object you may leave (section 9).

7. Helping you with requests

STK holds no row and nothing of your customers: a request from one of them about a conversation is one STK has nothing to answer with, and you answer it from your own rows. For your account's own data, write to izatai12@icloud.com.

8. Personal data breach

We post it in the Changelog without undue delay after becoming aware of a breach affecting your data, with what we know and what we are doing.

9. End of processing

STK receives and keeps no row. Each day that is billed something is an invoice item with Stripe, holding that day's two counts (permissions, people joining) and what was billed; with the invoices, Stripe keeps them as payment records, and STK does not delete them. Each report is deleted 7 years after it was made. Your account (the address and the hashed keys, with Stripe) is deleted 7 years after its last day with an ask or a person joining, or 30 days after it was made if its live key was never used: an account in use stays. The address stays on the invoices Stripe keeps. GET /v1/ledger gives your days' counts at any time. Stripe keeps its payment records as the law requires.

10. Information and audit

We answer written questions about this processing. GET /v1/ledger shows your account's days' counts; STK holds no row. GET /v1/rules?history=1 shows every rule used, with its date.

11. Law and courts

This agreement is governed by the laws of Japan. The Tokyo District Court has exclusive jurisdiction in the first instance.

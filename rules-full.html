STK permission rules

These rules are the same for every company. STK answers from these rules and from nothing else: not the
words of the conversation (STK never receives them), not a confidence number.

Each intent is one act. read: answer what is, and change nothing. change: change something. decide: make a
decision. A conversation goes to a person for two reasons only: a law names a person for its act, or a trigger
happened. An intent is on the Never list only when a law names a person for that act; the law, its section and
the address of its official text, as STK reads them, are written beside it below. These rules are the same for
every company, so where laws differ the strictest is followed. Every other intent is on the Permitted list.

Every intent below is of the domain conversation: an act of an AI in a conversation with a customer. Its full
name is conversation. followed by the name listed (conversation.claim.status); either form is read as the same.

1. trigger is not unknown: escalate. Pass unknown only when none of the Triggers below happened in the
   conversation; when one did, pass its name.
2. intent is other: escalate. other is a conversation that could not be told as any act, so it is not known what
   it would do.
3. intent is on the Never list: escalate.
4. intent is on no list (a name these rules do not have): escalate.
5. intent is on the Closed list: escalate.
6. Otherwise: finish.

Triggers (6), each one passed when it happened at any point in the conversation, whatever the intent:
- customer_asked: the customer asked for a person (a human, an agent, a representative, a supervisor, a manager), or said they do not want to deal with the AI, in any words, once.
- distressed: the customer said they are frightened, panicking, crying or cannot cope, or spoke of a medical emergency, of harming themselves, or of a danger to someone's life now.
- angry: the customer insulted or swore at the company, its staff or the AI, or said they are angry or furious.
- bereavement: the customer said that someone has died, or the conversation is about the account, the plan or the affairs of someone who has died.
- threat: the customer threatened harm to anyone or to property, or said someone is threatening or forcing them.
- legal_claim: the customer spoke of a lawyer, a lawsuit, a court, a regulator or an ombudsman, a formal complaint or a formal appeal, or of taking legal action.

Never (17): account.refuse, claim.deny_medical, claim.refuse, collections.refuse, coverage.deny_medical, coverage.refuse, credit.refuse, decision.appeal, decision.review, dispute.refuse, fraud.refuse, limit.lower, payment_arrangement.refuse, priorauth.deny_medical, priorauth.refuse, refund.refuse, transfer.error_refuse

Two industries, built differently

The two industries are built differently. In US health plan rules, the law names the person who must decide. In US federal financial rules, the law sets the duty to investigate, the deadline, and the notice, but does not name a person. For financial actions a person is named by European and British law and, in the United States, by two state laws from 2027-01-01: Colorado's (C.R.S. § 6-1-1705(1)(a)(II), meaningful human review of a consequential decision on request, a financial or lending service among them) and California's rules under the CCPA (11 CCR § 7221(b), an appeal to a human reviewer, which a business may offer in place of an opt-out, for financial and lending decisions). This rule puts an action on the person's side only where a person is named.

So each action below shows two things apart: the citation that names a person, which is why an action requires human review, and the clock and duties that attach to the action without naming a person.

Two locks

A conversation goes to a person for two reasons only: a law names a person for its act, or a trigger happened. STK's server does not start with a new version of these rules that breaks either lock.

  • Lock 1, the law. An intent is on the Never list only with a law that names a person for it: its section and the address of its official text, written beside it. An intent with no such law is not on the Never list. When that law stops naming a person, the version lists the intent under "No longer named by law" and permits it in that version. Every intent a version adds to the Permitted list is published with two reasons: it is not a refusal or a contest, and no law names a person for it, with the laws checked; and whether it was taken from other.
  • Lock 2. The Triggers list can only get longer. A new version may add a trigger, never take one off or change its words. A longer list can only lower what STK is paid.

So every intent is where it is because of a law or its absence, and anyone can check each one against the official text and the reasons published with it. Only STK makes a new version; no call does.

  • Additions, on the day they are made: an intent added to the Permitted list, a trigger added, a clause, reason or explanation added, an error corrected, a new domain added.

These rules are the same for every company. STK answers from these rules and from nothing else: not the

decision. A conversation goes to a person for two reasons only: a law names a person for its act, or a trigger
happened. An intent is on the Never list only when a law names a person for that act; the law, its section and
the address of its official text, as STK reads them, are written beside it below. These rules are the same for
every company, so where laws differ the strictest is followed. Every other intent is on the Permitted list.

Every intent below is of the domain conversation: an act of an AI in a conversation with a customer. Its full
name is conversation. followed by the name listed (conversation.claim.status); either form is read as the same.

1. trigger is not unknown: escalate. Pass unknown only when none of the Triggers below happened in the
   conversation; when one did, pass its name.
2. intent is other: escalate. other is a conversation that could not be told as any act, so it is not known what
   it would do.
3. intent is on the Never list: escalate.
4. intent is on no list (a name these rules do not have): escalate.
5. intent is on the Closed list: escalate.
6. Otherwise: finish.

- customer_asked: the customer asked for a person (a human, an agent, a representative, a supervisor, a manager), or said they do not want to deal with the AI, in any words, once.

- threat: the customer threatened harm to anyone or to property, or said someone is threatening or forcing them.

STK permission rules

These rules are the same for every company. STK answers from these rules and from nothing else: not the
words of the conversation (STK never receives them), not a confidence number.

Each intent is one act. read: answer what is, and change nothing. change: change something. decide: make a
decision. An intent is on the Never list only when a law names a person for that act; the law and its section,
as STK reads them, are written beside it below. These rules are the same for every company, so where laws
differ the strictest is followed. Every other intent is on the Permitted list. Whatever the intent, a trigger
is a customer's right to a person.

0. Your account has an invoice still unpaid 60 days after its due date: escalate, until it is paid.
1. trigger is not unknown: escalate. Pass unknown only when none of the Triggers below happened in the
   conversation; when one did, pass its name.
2. intent is on the Never list: escalate.
3. intent is not on the Never list, the Permitted list or the Closed list: escalate.
4. intent is on the Closed list: escalate.
5. Otherwise: finish.

- customer_asked: the customer asked for a person (a human, an agent, a representative, a supervisor, a manager), or said they do not want to deal with the AI, in any words, once.

- threat: the customer threatened harm to anyone or to property, or said someone is threatening or forcing them.

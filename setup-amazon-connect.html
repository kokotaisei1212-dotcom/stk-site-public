Where it goes

Amazon Connect

In the flow, an AWS Lambda function block before the transfer to the queue. The two lines go in that Lambda.

Where

  1. Make a Lambda function (Python or Node.js). Put stk.py or stk.mjs in its package, and set STK_KEY to your key in the function's environment variables.
  2. In the flow, before Transfer to queue, add the AWS Lambda function block (Integrate), Execution mode Synchronous, pointing to that Lambda.
  3. After it, Check contact attributes on $.External.decision: finish goes back to your bot; anything else, and the block's Error and Timeout branches, go on to Transfer to queue.

The Lambda (Python)

import stk
stk.ON_ROW = keep_line                                         # your function: each line to your own store

def lambda_handler(event, context):
    a = event["Details"]["ContactData"]["Attributes"]       # set by your flow: the intent your bot gave
    cid = event["Details"]["ContactData"]["ContactId"]
    return {"decision": stk.ask(a.get("intent", "other"), a.get("trigger", "unknown"), conversation_id=cid)}

When an agent takes the contact over, a Lambda on that path can call stk.joined(contact_id): a finish of it that day is taken off the bill. A Lambda is stopped when it returns: an answer from STK after 3 seconds may not be told to STK (Known limits).

Amazon Connect waits at most 8 seconds for a synchronous Lambda; stk.py itself stops waiting for STK at 3 seconds and returns escalate, unless a grant holds. A Lambda is stopped when it returns: call stk.flush() before it returns if seals may be waiting in the queue.

The file goes inside Amazon Connect's Lambda: it decides there and sends STK only the seal and its time. The contact id and the intent stay in your row, with you. Keep what stk.py writes in your own store with ON_ROW (S3, DynamoDB or another): what happens to a Lambda's files is not on the pages read, so do not count on them.

Not known

  • The handover inside Amazon Lex or Amazon Q in Connect: not read.

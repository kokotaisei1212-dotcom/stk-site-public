Review

The questions a security, privacy, legal or vendor-risk review asks, each with STK's answer. Each answer comes from STK's code or from another page of this site, linked beside it. Where STK has nothing, the answer says so.

What STK receives

Q. How many bytes does STK receive for one permission, and what are they?
A. The body of POST /v1/ask is {"seal": "<64 hex characters>", "recorded_ms": <13 digits>}: 106 bytes when stk.py sends it, 103 when stk.mjs sends it. The seal is 32 bytes, SHA-256 of 32 random bytes and the customer's row; recorded_ms is when the library recorded the action. A seal sent from the library's queue also carries the grant STK signed. A request with any other field is refused with 422. The key comes in the X-STK-Key header. The request carries what every HTTPS request carries: the address it comes from and its headers. One request as it reached STK, byte for byte: What we receive.

Q. What does STK not receive?
A. None of these reaches STK:

  • The words of the conversation, as text.
  • The audio of the conversation.
  • The customer's name.
  • A member number or any other id of the customer.
  • Medical or health information.
  • An account number.
  • The intent and the trigger of the conversation (the intent only if the company reports a permission with POST /v1/incident).

Q. How can STK sign without knowing what was decided?
A. The decision is made inside the company's own process. stk.py or stk.mjs reads STK's public rules, applies them to the intent and the trigger, and calls STK only when the rules answer finish. It then sends the seal of the row, not the row. STK signs the seal with the time and the version of the rules. SHA-256 cannot be run backwards, and the 32 random bytes stay with the company, so the seal tells STK nothing of the row. Anyone with the row and its random bytes can check that the row is the one sealed: How to check a row.

What STK keeps

Q. How long does STK keep a seal, and where?
A. A seal is not written down, unless the company reports it. In memory, the first 8 bytes of the SHA-256 of the account and the seal are kept for its day (UTC), so the same seal sent twice that day is counted once. No seal can be read back from them. They are let go 10 minutes after the day ends. The receipt STK returns is not kept by STK.

Q. Where does STK keep records?
A. In Stripe only. STK runs no store of its own. Stripe holds the account (email address, the keys' hashes), each billed day's counts and amount, the invoices, STK's public keys and the reports. Subprocessors

Q. Does STK hold the company's rows?
A. No. The rows and their random bytes are written where the company's code runs. STK never received them. If anyone asks STK for a conversation, a row or an intent, STK has none to give.

Personal data and health information

Q. Is STK a processor of personal data or of protected health information?
A. STK receives what is listed above, and only that. Whether that falls under your company's rules for personal data or protected health information is for your company's legal team to decide. STK does not offer a HIPAA business associate agreement; it does not have one today, and a company that concludes one is needed can write to izatai12@icloud.com. The account's email address is personal data, and the DPA covers it.

This is not legal advice.

Keys and signatures

Q. How are receipts signed?
A. Ed25519, with keys of one day (one key, or more if STK's server started again that day). The signature covers the receipt's fields in a fixed order, as a JSON array with no spaces. Anyone checks it with the public key of its day, from GET /v1/rules?keys=1, with no secret.

Q. When is a public key published?
A. Seven days ahead. STK signs only with keys whose public keys it has already written to Stripe. Every hour the public keys are copied into this site's public repository, and none is ever deleted. /v1/health says ok false when fewer than 3 days of keys are published ahead.

Q. What happens if a signing key leaks?
A. STK marks that key invalid in GET /v1/rules?keys=1, with the date, and lists it in GET /v1/rules?history=1. From then on no row signed with that key checks, and that day is billed nothing for every company. If STK signs again that day, it is with a new key. A person at STK must find out about the leak and run the command that marks the key invalid; nothing finds a leak by itself. Known limits

Q. What if a company's own key leaks?
A. STK cannot tell a leaked key from the company's. The company replaces its keys with POST /v1/keys, its account's email and its live key; the old keys stop at once. Until then, permissions signed with the leaked live key are billed to the company. Known limits

When STK stops

Q. What does the company's AI do when STK does not answer?
A. Without an answer within 300 ms, or on any error, stk.py and stk.mjs return escalate: the company's code hands the conversation to a person, as it does today. When STK cannot be reached, no receipt is made and nothing is billed. The one exception: while a grant from STK's last answer holds (10 minutes), they return finish for what the rules say finish, and send the seal when STK answers again; it is then signed and billed, and its receipt shows both times. When STK signs after the 300 ms, the file tells STK a person has the conversation, and that permission is not billed; if STK cannot be told then, the file keeps a to_tell line and tells STK as soon as it answers (after 00:10 UTC the day after, it stays billed). The conversation itself does not stop. What happens when

Q. Where can the state of STK be seen?
A. GET https://api.stk-evidence.com/v1/health, no key, answers {"ok": true} or false. A job in this site's public repository checks it; GitHub is asked to run it every hour and has run it 5 to 10 hours apart (11 times from 2026-11-18 04:59:35 to 2026-11-21 11:41:31 UTC). STK has no status page.

The rules

Q. Can the rules be set for one company?
A. No. One text, the same for every company. There is no setting per company. The rules

Q. Who can change them, and what is published when they change?
A. Only STK, in its code; no call changes them. A new version is written to the history with its date before it answers a call. Each version's full text, its SHA-256 and STK's signature are published at GET /v1/rules?history=1, and every change is in the Changelog. Every receipt names the version it was signed under.

Q. Why is an intent sent to a person?
A. For two reasons only: a law names a person for its act, or a trigger happened. The law, its section and the address of its official text are written beside each intent on the Never list, as STK reads them; that reading is not legal advice. A conversation that could not be told as any act (other) is sent to a person too. STK's server does not start with a version that puts an intent on the Never list without such a law, takes a trigger off or changes its words, or adds an intent to the Permitted list without publishing why: that it is not a refusal or a contest, which laws were checked, and whether it came from other. The rules

Q. Can STK check that the company's code gives the intent and the trigger truthfully?
A. No. STK does not see the conversation, so it cannot check what the company's code puts in the row. What STK signs is that this company declared this row, sealed, at this time, under this version of the rules. Whether the declaration was true is the responsibility of the company that made it. The company's own records keep exactly what was declared: the row, its random bytes and STK's receipt, which together show the intent and the trigger as declared. Known limits

Q. Can a person affected by an act of the company's AI check it?
A. Yes. With a receipt and its signature only, anyone can check that STK signed it, when, and under which version of the rules, without the network: A receipt and its signature only. With five things the company can give (the row, its random bytes, the receipt, the signature and the version of the rules), the person can check, asking neither the company nor STK, whether the row the company declared was permitted by the rules (what a receipt proves, and what it does not): What a company can give a person.

Price and terms

Q. What does STK cost?
A. Under the rules for conversations, $5 for each permission. A permission a person joins the same day (UTC) is not billed. Nothing else is charged. Price

Q. Why a card, and why Stripe?
A. Why a card, not an invoice · Why Stripe · What STK holds

Q. How is it billed and paid?
A. Both keys are given at once; the live key signs only after a card is registered on Stripe's page, and nothing is billed before. Each Monday Stripe makes the invoice for the week before (Monday to Sunday, UTC), due when issued, and charges it that day to that card, in US dollars. STK adds no tax to it. A declined card: Stripe tries again, and the invoice stays open until paid. An invoice still unpaid 60 days after it was issued stops STK signing for that account until it is paid.

Q. Is there a signed contract?
A. No. Taking a key means accepting the Terms and the DPA. What STK binds itself to is in those, in the published rules and their history, and in Known limits. STK offers no audit or inspection clause, no exit plan and no quantitative service level.

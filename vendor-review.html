Vendor review

Vendor review

What procurement, information security and legal look for first, on one page. Each answer is what STK's code and its other pages say. Where STK has nothing, the answer says so.

What STK receives

  • A 32-byte SHA-256 seal, sent as 64 hex characters, with the company's key in a header, and the time the company's library recorded the action. For a seal sent from the library's queue, also the grant STK signed. Nothing else: a request with any other field is refused.
  • The row and the 32 random bytes that produced the seal stay with the company. STK never has them.
  • STK has no database. Its records (the account, each billed day's counts, invoices, public keys, reports) are kept in Stripe.

What STK does not receive

  • The words of a conversation, as text or audio.
  • A name, an email address, a phone number, a member number, an account number or any other identifier of the company's customers.
  • Health or medical information.
  • A card number or a bank account number, of anyone. The company's own card is registered on Stripe's page and kept by Stripe.
  • The intent and the trigger. A report (the company's choice) carries the intent, and the jurisdictions if given, with POST /v1/incident.
  • A confidence number, the AI's name or version, or the row in any form.

Every HTTPS request also carries the address it comes from and its headers. STK writes neither down for POST /v1/ask.

Data protection

What STK receives is a hash made from a row and 32 random bytes that only the company holds. STK does not have the random bytes, so it has no means to recover the row from the hash. Whether that is personal data in a given jurisdiction is for the company's legal team to decide. This is not legal advice.

For a company whose process needs a data processing agreement, STK has one standard DPA. It is the same for every company and is not negotiated. It covers what STK does hold: the account's email address and the keys' hashes.

Business associate agreement: STK receives none of the health information listed above and does not offer a business associate agreement. Whether one is needed for a company's use of STK is that company's legal team's decision. STK does not have one today; a company that concludes one is needed can write to izatai12@icloud.com.

How STK's claims can be checked without one:

  • The rules are public, and each version is fixed by its SHA-256 and STK's signature.
  • Every receipt is signed with a public key published seven days before it signs.
  • The company keeps its own rows and can check each one, and STK's invoice, without STK: How to check a row.
  • stk.py, stk.mjs and stk_verify.py are files the company reads and runs on its own side.
  • What STK cannot do, or does not do, is written on Known limits; its mistakes are in the Changelog.

MSA

STK has one master services agreement, the same for every company and not negotiated. It is for a company whose process needs one. A key is given without it, as before.

If STK is gone

What stays with the company:

  • Its rows, their random bytes and STK's receipts, in the files stk.py and stk.mjs keep (or wherever its own ON_ROW function writes them).
  • In the same files, the signed text of each version of the rules applied and the public key of each receipt, saved by the library as it read them.
  • STK's public keys in the public site repository (keys.json), and every version of the rules on The rules.
  • stk_verify.py, a file the company already has.

The check needs nothing of STK's: python3 stk_verify.py --from-file <the file> reads the keys and the rules from the file itself and checks each permission's seal, signature and rules with no network. How to check a row

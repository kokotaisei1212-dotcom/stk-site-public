What we receive

What we receive

One permission, as it reached STK, byte for byte. Only the key is cut short here.

STK never sees the conversation. Only a 32-byte hash reaches us.

User-Agent: stk.py

Connection: close

{"seal": "f3fbd2a623e64bd72d2c223dcd44116f7b40335097adf239375bdf492a613382", "recorded_ms": 1795229771677}

Received by api.stk-evidence.com on 2026-11-21 02:56:11 UTC, sent by stk.py with a test key. The key's characters after its first nine are left out here.

What STK receives

The seal32 bytes, sent as 64 hex characters: SHA-256 of 32 random bytes and your row. A call with any other field is refused (422).
When your library recorded itrecorded_ms: your library's own time of the action, your claim. For a seal that waited in the library's queue, also the grant STK signed, which the library sends back.
Your keyIn X-STK-Key: whose permission it is, and whether it is billed (live) or not (test).
The timeSTK's own clock when the seal arrives. It is in the signed receipt, beside recorded_ms.
A person joiningPOST /v1/joined: a count, with no body or with the seal of the permission it is for.
At signupThe email address the month's email goes to. The keys come back once; STK keeps only their hashes. The bank account is registered on Stripe's page and kept by Stripe; STK never receives its number.

The request also carries what every HTTPS request carries: the address it comes from, and the headers shown above. STK writes neither down for POST /v1/ask.

What STK does not receive

The words of the conversation, in text or audio. The intent (only if you report a permission: POST /v1/incident). The trigger. A conversation id. Your customer's name, email address, phone number, account number or any other id. A confidence number. Your AI's name or version. The row in any form.

Why nothing comes back out of the seal

SHA-256 cannot be run backwards. A row has few possible values (an intent, a trigger), so the seal includes 32 random bytes that stay with you: to find a row from its seal, one would have to try 2256 random values for each guess. Without the random bytes, the seal says nothing about the row; with them, anyone can check that a row is the one sealed.

What STK keeps, and for how long

Of a permission: nothing written down, unless you report it. The receipt is not kept. In memory, the first 8 bytes of the SHA-256 of your account and the seal are kept for its day (UTC), from which no seal can be read back, so the same seal sent twice that day is counted once; they are let go 10 minutes after the day ends.

Of your account: the email address and the keys' hashes, with Stripe, until 7 years after its last day with an ask or a person joining (30 days after it was made, if its live key was never used); each billed day's count of permissions and of people joining, and what was billed, on Stripe's invoices, which Stripe keeps as payment records. Each report you make: its date and the intent you give with it, public, and the seal of the permission reported, with Stripe, 7 years.

Who else sees it

Stripe: your email address and the keys' hashes, each day's count and amount, the invoices, STK's public keys and the reports. Render runs STK's server, so the requests pass through it. Render serves this site. Resend sends STK's one email a month and keeps each for 30 days. Nobody else. The site loads nothing from any other company: no font, script or style; a test checks every page of it before it is published. Subprocessors

Where the conversation is

With you only: your row and its 32 random bytes are written where your code runs (stk-rows, or your own store). STK never had them.

If anyone asks STK to disclose a conversation, a row or an intent of yours, STK has nothing to give: it never received one. What it has is listed above.

The rules

Credit refused

conversation.credit.refuse: Refuse a loan, a card or credit the individual asked for.

Human review required: a law names a person for this action.

The law that names a person

GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679) Art. 22(1) and (3); UK GDPR Arts. 22A and 22C

Its official text:

  • https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj
  • https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/section/80

The law's own words are human intervention (UK GDPR Art. 22C, in force from 2026-02-05 by SI 2026/82; for credit in the EU, Art. 18(8) of Directive (EU) 2023/2225, applied from 2026-11-20). In health plans the same is called physician review, Medical Director review or peer-to-peer; in financial services, refer to underwriter, second-level review or manual review. The laws are STK's reading, not legal advice. Where laws differ, the strictest is followed.

Clock and duties

What attaches to the action without naming a person, as read in each primary source:

  • Notice of action taken: within 30 days after a completed application (12 CFR 1002.9(a)(1)(i))
  • Where a creditworthiness assessment uses automated processing, the consumer's right to request and obtain human intervention, applied from 2026-11-20 (EU) (Directive (EU) 2023/2225 Art. 18(8), Art. 48(1))

Names by industry

  • Banks, card issuers, lenders, insurers: refer to underwriter

Two industries, built differently

The two industries are built differently. In US health plan rules, the law names the person who must decide. In US federal financial rules, the law sets the duty to investigate, the deadline, and the notice, but does not name a person. For financial actions a person is named by European and British law and, in the United States, by two state laws from 2027-01-01: Colorado's (C.R.S. § 6-1-1705(1)(a)(II), meaningful human review of a consequential decision on request, a financial or lending service among them) and California's rules under the CCPA (11 CCR § 7221(b), an appeal to a human reviewer, which a business may offer in place of an opt-out, for financial and lending decisions). This rule puts an action on the person's side only where a person is named.

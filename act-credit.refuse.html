The rules

Credit refused

conversation.credit.refuse: Refuse a loan, a card or credit the individual asked for.

Human review required: a law names a person for this action.

The law that names a person

GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679) Art. 22(1) and (3); UK GDPR Arts. 22A and 22C (in force from 2026-02-05: Data (Use and Access) Act 2025, s. 80 and Sch. 6, by SI 2026/82, reg. 2(j), (z8); not for a decision taken before that day, reg. 5); Directive (EU) 2023/2225 Art. 18(8) (credit agreements for consumers: where a creditworthiness assessment uses automated processing, the consumer's right to request and obtain human intervention; applied through each EU Member State's law transposing the Directive, from 2026-11-20); C.R.S. § 6-1-1705(1)(a)(II) (Colorado SB 26-189: on the consumer's request, the deployer provides an opportunity for meaningful human review and reconsideration of the consequential decision, to the extent commercially reasonable; definitions in § 6-1-1701(15); applies to decisions made on or after 2027-01-01; enforcement stayed by the order of 2026-04-27 in X.AI LLC v. Weiser, No. 1:26-cv-01515 (D. Colo.); the attorney general's rules, 4 CCR 904-6, not in effect); 11 CCR § 7221(a), (b)(1)(A) (California, regulations under the CCPA: the business provides the consumer with a method to appeal the decision to a human reviewer who has the authority to overturn the decision; for decisions on financial or lending services, § 7001(ddd)(1); from 2027-01-01; not automated decisionmaking technology where the human involvement of § 7001(e)(1) is present; decisions of a financial institution on consumers' nonpublic personal information may be outside it by the exemption for GLBA data in Cal. Civ. not where s. 35(2) applies); Nigeria Data Protection Act, 2023 s. 37(1) (in force 2023-06-12: a right not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing of personal data, including profiling, which produces legal or similar significant effects; not where s. 37(2) applies; s. 37(3), the right to obtain human intervention); Data Protection Act 2017 (Mauritius) s. 38(1) (in force 2018-01-15: a right not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing, including profiling, which produces legal effects or significantly affects the person; not where s. 38(2) applies); Federal Law No. 152-FZ, Art. 16(1)-(2) (Russia, On Personal Data: a decision producing legal consequences or otherwise affecting rights and lawful interests is not taken on the sole basis of automated processing, save with written consent or where a federal law so provides); Loi n° 78-17, art. 47 (France: no decision producing legal effects or significantly affecting a person is taken on the sole basis of automated processing, including profiling, save the cases of GDPR Art. 22(2)(a) and (c)); Ley N° 21.719, art. primero, Ley N° 19.628 art. 8° bis (Chile, from 2027-10-15 by Ley N° 21.855: a right not to be subject to decisions based on automated processing, including profiling, with legal or significant effects; not in the cases of its letters a, b and c; in every case the right to human intervention and review); POPIA s. 71(1) (South Africa, Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013, in force 2020-07-01: no decision with legal consequences or affecting the person to a substantial degree based solely on automated processing intended to profile, including performance at work, credit worthiness, reliability, location, health, personal preferences or conduct; not where s. 71(2) applies); Loi n° 09-08, art. 11 (Morocco: no decision producing legal effects is taken on the sole basis of automated processing intended to define a profile or evaluate aspects of personality; not a decision in a contract where the person could present observations, nor one satisfying the person's requests); Ley N° 29733, art. 23 (Peru: a right not to be subject to a decision with legal or significant effects based only on processing intended to evaluate aspects of personality or conduct; not in the negotiation, conclusion or performance of a contract, or an evaluation for joining a public entity); Ley N° 18.331, art. 16 (Uruguay, as worded by Ley N° 18.719 from 2011-01-01: a right not to be subject to a decision with legal effects that significantly affects the person, based on automated processing intended to evaluate aspects of personality, such as work performance, credit, reliability or conduct)

Each law: where, from when, and its kind

From the signed rules: the jurisdiction of each law, the day written in it, and its kind as the rules' list of three kinds names it. With no day written, a law applies from the day it is cited. Given jurisdictions, this action goes to a person only in those listed here; in any other, an AI may finish it, if that jurisdiction's laws on its subject have been read; a jurisdiction not read is answered as with none given (from Rule 16). With none given, it goes to a person.

The law's own words are human intervention (UK GDPR Art. 22C, in force from 2026-02-05 by SI 2026/82). In health plans the same is called physician review, Medical Director review or peer-to-peer; in financial services, refer to underwriter, second-level review or manual review. The laws are STK's reading, not legal advice. Where laws differ, the strictest is followed.

Clock and duties

What attaches to the action without naming a person, as read in each primary source:

  • Notice of action taken: within 30 days after a completed application (12 CFR 1002.9(a)(1)(i))
  • Where a creditworthiness assessment uses automated processing, the consumer's right to request and obtain human intervention, under each EU Member State's law transposing the Directive, which the Directive requires to apply from 2026-11-20 (EU) (Directive (EU) 2023/2225 Art. 18(8), Art. 48(1))

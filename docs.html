Plug STK in

  1. Get your keys: enter your email on the first page, or POST /v1/keys. Both keys are shown once, there and then; the live key signs once you register a card on Stripe's page. STK keeps only their hashes. It sends one email only, and not for this: from 2026-12-05 12:00 UTC, a notice to your account's address when new keys are asked for it without a key and with a card other than its own (how).
  2. Put stk.py (or stk.mjs) next to your AI's code, and set the environment variable STK_KEY to your key (never write it into the file).
  3. Where your code is about to hand a conversation to a person: if stk.ask_where(intent, trigger, "US-TX", conversation_id=cid)["answer"] == "finish": your AI goes on; else it hands the conversation over, as it does today. The jurisdiction (codes) is kept in your row and changes no answer: stk.ask(intent, trigger, conversation_id=cid) answers the same.
  4. When a person joins a conversation that got finish: stk.joined(cid). Told the same day (UTC), or by 00:10 UTC the day after, that permission is not billed. If STK cannot be told, joined() returns all the same: the file keeps a to_tell line and tells STK as soon as it answers, also after a restart. Told after 00:10 UTC the day after, that permission stays billed.

The file decides, on your side, from STK's public rules. Only on finish does it call STK, and it sends the seal and the time it recorded the action, and nothing else. Without an answer within 3 seconds, or on any error, ask() returns escalate, unless the last grant holds: then it returns finish and sends the seal from its queue when STK answers again (POST /v1/ask). stk.flush() (stk.py) or await flush() (stk.mjs) waits until the queue is sent; call it before a process ends. An answer of STK that came too late to be used is not billed. STK bills nothing it took more than 3 seconds to answer; an answer your code stopped waiting for is told to STK by the file, at once or, if your process ended first, when it starts again.

The answer is the same whatever jurisdiction is given, or none: the line read on 2026-11-25, which does not change. When it is escalate, ask_where returns because, the jurisdictions whose laws send the action to a person.

The first ask() of a process reads the rules first, for up to 2 seconds; the 3 seconds are counted after that.

Try it without a key; it reads the public rules and sends nothing of yours:

curl -s https://stk-evidence.com/stk.py | python3 - conversation.balance.inquiry unknown
import stk   # reads your key from STK_KEY
r = stk.ask_where(intent, trigger, "US-TX", conversation_id=cid)   # kept in the row; the answer is the same without it
if r["answer"] == "finish":
    ...   # your AI goes on
else:
    ...   # hand it to a person
# the same without a jurisdiction: stk.ask(intent, trigger, conversation_id=cid)
import { askWhere } from "./stk.mjs";   // reads your key from STK_KEY
const r = await askWhere(intent, trigger, "US-TX", { conversationId: cid });   // kept in the row; the answer is the same without it
if (r.answer === "finish") {
  // your AI goes on
} else {
  // hand it to a person
}
// the same without a jurisdiction: await ask(intent, trigger, { conversationId: cid })

Get keys

On the first page, or with POST /v1/keys below.

Your code may hand any conversation to a person: STK never requires a finish. If STK cannot answer, your AI hands the conversation to a person, unless a grant holds. STK never sees the conversation.

MCP

For an AI client that speaks MCP (Model Context Protocol). Free, no key:

{ "mcpServers": {
    "stk": { "command": "npx", "args": ["-y", "stk-mcp"] } } }

Only when you need a signature, add your key:

{ "mcpServers": {
    "stk": { "command": "npx", "args": ["-y", "stk-mcp"], "env": { "STK_KEY": "your key" } } } }

Three tools: stk_where and stk_actions, free and with no key, send STK nothing; stk_ask, with STK_KEY, sends only the seal; each action an AI finishes alone is billed, $0 when a person must decide (Price). Each permission is kept in ~/.stk-mcp/rows/<day>.jsonl: the row (the action, the trigger, the jurisdiction and your content), its random bytes and STK's receipt; STK never has them.

trigger

unknownNone of the triggers below happened in the conversation. The only trigger the rules can answer finish to.
customer_askedthe customer asked for a person (a human, an agent, a representative, a supervisor, a manager), or said they do not want to deal with the AI, in any words, once.
distressedthe customer said they are frightened, panicking, crying or cannot cope, or spoke of a medical emergency, of harming themselves, or of a danger to someone's life now.
angrythe customer insulted or swore at the company, its staff or the AI, or said they are angry or furious.
bereavementthe customer said that someone has died, or the conversation is about the account, the plan or the affairs of someone who has died.
threatthe customer threatened harm to anyone or to property, or said someone is threatening or forcing them.
legal_claimthe customer spoke of a lawyer, a lawsuit, a court, a regulator or an ombudsman, a formal complaint or a formal appeal, or of taking legal action.

intent is one of the 91 ids in The rules in full (each on its page from The rules), by its full name (conversation.order.status) or the name listed (order.status). Pass other for a conversation that could not be told as any act: the rules answer escalate (other). Any name not on the lists is answered escalate.

The row and the seal

The row is yours, in any shape: intent and trigger are all it must have. ask() puts in it whatever else you give it (stk.ask(intent, trigger, conversation_id=cid, ticket=991)), as JSON written one way only, the same in stk.py and stk.mjs: keys sorted by code point, no spaces, Unicode as it is, numbers as JavaScript writes them, NaN and infinities as null. It makes 32 random bytes, and the seal: SHA-256 of the random bytes followed by the row. The seal goes to STK; the row and the random bytes stay with you. Nothing can be read back from the seal.

Your rows are only on your side. STK holds not one of them. Keep the files the library saved.

A conversation and intent that got finish today (UTC) get finish again without calling STK (on the same server): only a call to STK is a permission, and billed.

Jurisdictions

stk.ask_where(intent, trigger, jurisdiction, conversation_id=cid) (stk.mjs: askWhere(intent, trigger, jurisdiction, { conversationId: cid })) takes a code or a list of codes, ISO 3166-1 for a country (US, GB), ISO 3166-2 for a part of one (US-WA), or EU; UK is read as GB. It returns {"answer": "finish" or "escalate", "read": true or false, "status": "read", "none found", "not checked" or "not published", "because": [codes]}. From the rules' seventeenth version the answer is the same whatever codes are given, or none: an intent on the Never list goes to a person when a law beside it applies on the day. The Checked list of the rules is empty, so status is not checked for every code; it is written in the row as jurisdiction_status, beside jurisdiction. When the answer is escalate, because lists the jurisdictions whose laws send the intent to a person. This line was read on 2026-11-25, in every jurisdiction read that day, and does not change. Under earlier versions the codes could change the answer; the rules' history keeps each version.

An action the rules do not publish is treated as requiring a person (escalate; with ask_where, status "not published"), and STK claims nothing about it.

The codes are kept in your row, as jurisdiction, and sealed with it; STK still receives only the seal. ask() is unchanged, and so is POST /v1/ask.

An intent on no list of the rules is answered escalate and leaves a not_listed line next to your rows, with the name your code gave; STK is not told. When those lines grow, your names have moved away from STK's.

Reference

Base: https://api.stk-evidence.com. Your key goes in the header X-STK-Key. Bodies are JSON. These eleven calls are all of STK's API; a twelfth address takes Stripe's events only.

POST /v1/askThe seal of a finish your code decided. One call is one permission, and is billed.
POST /v1/joinedA person joined a conversation.
GET /v1/rulesThe Rule in use, signed by STK. No key.
GET /v1/ledgerYour account's days, one row each.
POST /v1/incidentOne permission of your live key that went wrong.
POST /v1/keysYour test key and a card page; then your live key, once. No key.
GET /v1/healthWhether STK can sign as it should. No key.
POST /v1/keys/revokeEvery key of your account stops at once. Either key.
POST /v1/keys/reissueNew keys without a key: your account's card proves the account. No key.
POST /v1/keys/cancelNew keys asked with another card do not start. The live key.
POST /v1/keys/cardA new card for your account. The live key.

POST /v1/ask

A finish your code decided from the rules. The body is {"seal": "<64 hex characters>", "recorded_ms": <when your library recorded the action, ms since 1970 UTC>}, and for a seal that waited in the library's queue also "grant" and "grant_sig", the grant it finished under. Nothing else: any other field is refused with 422. One call is one permission, and is billed. The same seal again the same day is signed again and not counted again. Answer:

{"receipt": {"v": 2, "seal", "time_ms", "recorded_ms", "rule_version", "account", "which_key", "kid"},
 "sig": "<Ed25519 signature of the receipt, base64>",
 "rule_version": "<SHA-256 of the rules in use>",
 "rule": {"number": 17, "version": "<the same SHA-256>", "published": "<its date>"},
 "grant": {"v": 1, "account", "rule_version", "kid", "issued_ms", "expires_ms"},
 "grant_sig": "<Ed25519 signature of the grant, base64>"}

Two times in the receipt. time_ms: when STK signed, what STK attests. recorded_ms: when your library recorded the action, your own claim. They are the same moment unless the seal waited in the queue. Without a grant, a recorded_ms more than a minute from STK's clock is refused with 422 and STK's time in the X-STK-Time-Ms header; stk.py and stk.mjs then use STK's time and send once again. A body with the seal alone, as before, is signed with a receipt of version 1: {"v": 1, "seal", "time_ms", "rule_version", "account", "which_key", "kid"}, one time only.

The grant: for this account and this version of the rules, from issued_ms for 1 hour, never past the next April 15 or October 15, 00:00 UTC (the only days a restriction takes effect), nor past the moment the account would be unpaid 60 days, signed by STK over ["stk-grant", v, account, rule_version, kid, issued_ms, expires_ms] as JSON with no spaces. While it holds and STK cannot be reached, the libraries finish what the rules say finish and keep the seal in a queue (at most 10,000 seals; past it, escalate), then send it. They keep the grant in the rows folder (a grant line, at most every 5 minutes) with the rules it names, so a process started again while STK cannot be reached finishes under it too; with ON_ROW or setOnRow, it is kept in memory only. A grant STK did not sign for this account, or a recorded_ms outside it, is refused with 422.

Not signed, with "receipt": null, "sig": null and not_signed saying why: an invoice of your account unpaid 60 days after its due date (the Terms), STK without a published signing key for today, STK unable to sign just now (it cannot read its records in Stripe), a limit for the day (UTC) reached: STK signs up to 20,000,000 permissions a day for a live key (10,000 until an invoice of its account has been paid), 1,000 for a test key; or as many seals today, of all accounts, as STK can hold for a day (129,138,682). Nothing is billed; the file returns escalate.

When rule_version is not the Rule your file applied, the file reads the rules again and applies them again; if they now say escalate, it returns escalate and calls POST /v1/joined, so that permission is not billed.

POST /v1/joined

A person joined a conversation. No body, {}, or {"seal": "<64 hex characters>"}: the seal of the permission the person took over, which STK already has; anything else is refused with 422. It is a count: one permission taken off the bill of a day (UTC), never below 0. With the seal of a permission signed the day before, in the first 10 minutes after that day ended: that day (both days, when STK's server started again after the day before began). Otherwise: today. stk.py and stk.mjs send the seal when they have it. Answer: {"counted": true, "day": "2026-11-17"}, the day it counted for.

GET /v1/rules

No key. The Rule in use, signed by STK: {"number", "version", "published", "change", "summary", "text", "kid", "sig"}. number goes 1, 2, 3 with no gap; version is the SHA-256 of text. The signature is Ed25519 over ["stk-rules", version, published, text] as JSON with no spaces. When STK cannot sign just now, kid and sig are null: the text is the same, unsigned.

?history=1: every version the rules have had (each signed the same way), every report and every signing key made invalid, oldest first: {"versions": [...], "reports": [{"report_id", "reported", "intent"}], "keys_made_invalid": [...]}. ?history=1&format=csv: every report, as CSV.

?keys=1: every public key STK has made, one or more a day, published up to 7 days before its day, never deleted; a copy is at /keys.json: {"keys": [{"kid", "day", "public_key", "algorithm": "Ed25519", "invalid", "invalid_note"}]}. ?keys=1&kid=<kid>: that key only (stk.py and stk.mjs save it this way beside your lines).

GET /v1/ledger

Your account's days, one row each: {"days": [{"day": "2026-11-17", "finish": 412, "human_joined": 9, "billed": 403, "sent": true}]}. Beside them, "events": every time your account's keys stopped or were made again, a request for new keys with another card, its cancel or its start, a card changed, and the one email, each with its time, what happened ("detail", naming a card only by brand and last 4 digits) and "outcome": {"at": "2026-11-21T15:02:11Z", "event": "keys stopped", "detail": "with the live key: both keys stopped; …", "outcome": "done"}. Each event is written to Stripe before the change it records. While new keys asked with another card wait, "notice" says so. finish: calls to POST /v1/ask signed; human_joined: calls to POST /v1/joined. Each day's numbers are written to Stripe every 2 minutes while they change. "sent": true: the day is final, written the last time 10 minutes after it ended (UTC); "sent": false: today, or the day before until then. On a day STK's server started again, finish is the larger of the counts before and after the start. A day missing has no permission and no person joining, or was billed 0 in a week already invoiced. ?from=2026-11-01&to=2026-11-17, dates (UTC), both included; a year at most; without them, the last 31 days. 60 calls an hour for one account; then 429.

Check the invoice with your own lines

  1. Gather the week's lines (Monday to Sunday, UTC) from every server's stk-rows folder, or from the store your ON_ROW function writes to.
  2. Check each with stk_verify.py. Leave out any that does not check.
  3. Keep those of your own account with which_key: live.
  4. For each day (UTC, by each receipt's time_ms, not your server's clock), count F: your lines with a receipt; and H: your joined lines (the file writes one each time it tells STK a person has a conversation; one of the first 10 minutes of a day counts for the day before when that day has a permission not yet taken off).
  5. For each day, max(0, F − H); 0 for a day whose signing key was made invalid. On a day STK's server started again, the invoice can count less (see GET /v1/ledger above), never more. The week's sum is the most the invoice counts, less any credit note.
  6. Put your days beside GET /v1/ledger's. A day where the invoice counts more than your lines: send that day's lines (receipts and joined lines) to izatai12@icloud.com.

POST /v1/incident

{"receipt": {...}, "sig": "...", "intent": "..."}: the receipt and signature of one permission of your live key, and the intent it was for. Leaves one dated report, public in GET /v1/rules?history=1 with its intent and date, never your name. It changes no rule and no answer. STK cannot see that the intent is the sealed row's: you say so. From the rules' ninth version it may also carry "jurisdiction", the codes you gave ask_where: the intent is then read for them. Without it, nothing changes. A receipt that is not STK's, not your account's or not of your live key, or an intent the receipt's rules do not answer finish, is refused with 422. The same receipt again gives the same report. 10 reports in 24 hours for one account; then 429. Answer: {"report_id", "reported", "intent", "already_reported"}; already_reported is true for the same receipt again.

POST /v1/keys

No key. One call: {"email": "you@example.com"}; the answer is {"test_key": "stk_test_…", "live_key": "stk_live_…", "card_url": "https://checkout.stripe.com/…", "note": "…"}. Both keys are shown once. The test key works at once and is never billed. The live key signs once a card is registered on card_url, Stripe's page (nothing is charged there; STK never sees the card's number); before that, a call with it is answered not_signed and nothing is billed. When the page is done, Stripe sends you back to stk-evidence.com; nothing more needs to be sent, and the live key's first call after it finds the card. Its invoices are charged to that card each Monday. The card page lost: {"email": "you@example.com"} again with either key in X-STK-Key gives a new card_url, the keys unchanged. The test key gets the same answers as the live key, marked which_key: test; nothing asked with it is billed. The address is taken without spaces around it, in lower case, with its domain as the DNS has it. The same address again: 409, unless the call carries that account's live key in X-STK-Key (then two new keys at once, on the same card, and the old ones stop at once), or the keys have never been used and were made at least 10 minutes before (then a new test key and a new card page). With the card page's session ({"email", "card": "cs_…"} and a key of the address), the card is made the account's at once; 409 if the page was not done or not this address's. A call with the live key is how a leaked key is replaced. When STK cannot write to Stripe: 503, and no key was made. 10 an hour from one address on the internet; then 429, with the minutes to wait in the message and Retry-After. New accounts: 300 an hour in all; then 429 for a new address (replacing keys is not held up).

Pay. On the first page, once your keys are shown: Register a card, or Pay by invoice (the same call as below, made for you). The same call may carry "pay": card (left out: as every key taken before) or invoice. With invoice no card page is given and the live key signs at once; each month (UTC) is on one invoice in dollars, sent to the account's address on the 1st, due in 30 days, paid on the invoice's page by card or from a US bank account (ACH). With your live key in X-STK-Key, {"email": ..., "pay": "invoice"} moves an account to invoices and {"email": ..., "pay": "card"} gives a card page, after which each week is charged to the card; the keys stay. Without a card, new keys without a key (POST /v1/keys/reissue) cannot be had: keep the live key. An account paying by invoice that has paid an invoice by its due date has no limit: STK signs nothing for it only while one of its invoices is past its due date, and the answer says how much paying lets it sign again. One that has not yet paid an invoice may owe up to $5,000 (its open invoices and its month so far): at 80% of it, its days so far are invoiced at once, due in 30 days, and its address is emailed; at the limit, nothing is signed until a payment is confirmed. Stopped: POST /v1/ask answers unsigned with not_signed saying why, and the libraries answer escalate. A payment of an account disputed (a chargeback): the same answer for every key of the account, until the dispute is withdrawn or decided for STK. Decided against STK: the account is sent one invoice of the amount the bank took back (due in 30 days, card or US bank account), and once it is paid the keys sign again by themselves. There is no way to read what is left of the limit: at 80% of it STK emails the account's address, once for that limit and month (from 2026-12-08 12:00 UTC).

Use. The same call may carry "use", any word: it is accepted and kept as information, and the price does not change ($5 for every key). With your live key in X-STK-Key and a use, the call gives another test key and live key on the same account, card and invoice, as often as asked (up to 20 such pairs an account): {"test_key", "live_key", "use", "price_cents", "note"}. A pair's live key signs; it does not change the account (its card, how it pays, its keys). New keys for the account stop every key before, the pairs among them.

POST /v1/keys/revoke

Either key in X-STK-Key, no body. Every key of your account stops at once, and a request for new keys still waiting (below) is cancelled. Your account, its card, its invoices and its days stay. Nothing signed after this is billed; what was signed before is billed as usual, as STK cannot tell which of it was not yours. Answer: {"keys": "stopped", "note": "…"}. It is in GET /v1/ledger as keys stopped. For new keys: POST /v1/keys/reissue.

POST /v1/keys/reissue

No key. For keys lost, leaked and stopped, or never kept. First {"email": "you@example.com"}: the answer is {"card_url": "…"}, a page of Stripe's where you register your account's card (nothing is charged). The page brings you back to this page with its session in the address (?for=reissue&card=cs_…); send it with the address: {"email": "you@example.com", "card": "cs_…"}.

  • The account's own card (the same card, by Stripe's fingerprint): {"test_key": "…", "live_key": "…"} at once, and the keys before stop.
  • Another card, or an account with no card: new keys that start 7 days later, with "starts". Until then they do not work and the keys before do, and every answer to the keys before carries "notice" saying so. One call to POST /v1/keys/cancel with the live key before stops them. Not cancelled: on that day the new keys start, the keys before stop, and that card is the account's. STK also sends one email to the account's address about it (from 2026-12-05 12:00 UTC; before that, none is sent, and the ledger says so). STK sends one other email, at 80% of an invoice account's limit (Pay); none else.

A page is taken once, and only within a day of being made. One request with another card can wait at a time. Each step is in GET /v1/ledger.





POST /v1/keys/cancel


The live key in X-STK-Key, no body. New keys asked with another card (above) do not start; your keys stay as they are. Answer: {"cancelled": true}; 409 when none is waiting; 403 with the test key.



POST /v1/keys/card


The live key in X-STK-Key. First {}: {"card_url": "…"}, a page of Stripe's for the new card (nothing is charged). It brings you back to this page with ?for=card&card=cs_…; send {"card": "cs_…"} with the live key. The answer names the card ("visa ending 4242"): it is your account's from then on and its invoices are charged to it, and the change is in GET /v1/ledger. A card changed this way is your account's own card for new keys.

GET /v1/health


No key: {"ok": true}. ok is false when STK cannot sign as it should, or something it keeps in Stripe or sends there did not go as it should, or for 24 hours after its server stopped unexpectedly a second time in 250 days (a start of the same commit again: a crash, a kill, a restart; a deploy is not a stop). While a starting server has not read its records yet: {"ok": false} with 503.

Errors





401No X-STK-Key, or not a key.


422A field missing, not of the allowed form, or a field that is not allowed.


429POST /v1/keys and POST /v1/keys/reissue: more than 3 calls from one address in an hour (a call with one of your keys is not counted), or more than 300 new accounts in all in an hour; the message says when it opens again (in minutes, and the time in UTC), and Retry-After gives it in seconds. GET /v1/ledger: 60 calls of your account this hour. POST /v1/incident: 10 reports of your account in 24 hours. Any call: more than 120 calls a minute from one address with a key that is not one of STK's.


413A body over 16,384 bytes.


409POST /v1/keys: this address has keys; send its live key to make new ones (keys never used: again, 10 minutes after they were made).


400POST /v1/stripe/webhook: not signed by Stripe.


503STK cannot do this just now (it cannot read its records in Stripe), with Retry-After: 60: GET /v1/ledger, POST /v1/keys, GET /v1/rules?history=1 or ?keys=1, and calls with a key; also POST /v1/ask while a starting server has not read them, or when STK has not read from Stripe for 2 hours whether the account is unpaid. stk.py and stk.mjs take a 503 or a 429 as STK not reached: a grant that holds covers it.


500A fault in STK. ask() returns escalate.

Known limits

Known limits

What STK cannot do today, or can get wrong. Each one is still true.

Why your AI hands a conversation over

What happens. The reason is only the trigger your code puts in the row, and STK never sees it. If your code gives unknown for a conversation in which one of the triggers of The rules happened (the customer asked for a person, is distressed or angry, spoke of a death, a threat or a legal claim), and the intent is permitted, the rules say finish.

Who pays. You, in that conversation.

What you can do. Send unknown only when none of the triggers happened.

Your own names for intents

What happens. STK knows only its own 91 intent ids. stk.py and stk.mjs take spaces off around a name and read it in lower case; any other name is answered escalate, so a name of your own never gets a permission. Each time, the file keeps a not_listed line with the name, next to your rows; STK is not told. When those lines grow, your names have moved away from STK's.

Who pays. Nobody is billed; your AI gets nothing from STK for it.

What you can do. Send STK's own intent ids (the list).

A person joined a permitted conversation

What happens. STK knows a person joined only when your code calls stk.joined() (POST /v1/joined, a count). Without it, the permission is billed. Told after 00:10 UTC the day after the permission, it takes nothing off that day: the permission stays billed.

Who pays. You.

What you can do. Call stk.joined() when the person joins, the same day (UTC).

STK does not know how a conversation ended

What happens. STK does not see whether a customer came back after a permitted conversation. A permitted intent stays permitted: the line was read on 2026-11-25 and does not change. A report does not take it off.

Who pays. You, in those conversations.

What you can do. Check your conversations against what you kept; stop calling STK for an intent whenever you choose. Report a finish that went wrong: POST /v1/incident, dated and public.

A report closes nothing

What happens. A report is a dated, public row. No report and no version moves an intent to the other side: the line was read on 2026-11-25 and does not change.

Who pays. Nobody is billed for an intent that is not permitted: the file does not call STK for it.

A leaked key works until you replace it

What happens. STK cannot tell a leaked key from yours. Until you replace it, a leaked live key can get as many permissions signed as it asks for, billed to you at $5 each: a live key has no limit.

What you can do. To replace your keys, leaked or not: POST /v1/keys with your account's email and your live key in X-STK-Key. The new keys come back once; the old ones stop at once. If your live key itself leaked, whoever has it can do the same.

STK cannot give a row back

What happens. STK never receives a row: only its seal and the time it was recorded. stk.py or stk.mjs keeps each line (the row, its random bytes, STK's receipt) where your code runs. What you lose, or never kept, is gone: nobody has a copy, and GET /v1/ledger gives only each day's counts.

What you can do. Keep the folder stk.py and stk.mjs write to (stk-rows), or give them a function that puts each line in your own store. Compare your lines each day with GET /v1/ledger: a day with fewer lines than its count is a day you lost some.

A signing key that leaks

What happens. Each day has two signing keys of its own: the second signs only once the first is made invalid. Their private halves are kept in Stripe, encrypted with a key that is only in Render's environment, and STK signs with a day's keys only on that day. If a day's key leaks (from Stripe and Render's environment together, or from the server's memory), someone could make rows that check as STK's for that day. STK then marks that key invalid in GET /v1/rules?keys=1, with the date, and lists it in GET /v1/rules?history=1. From then on no row signed with that key checks with STK's keys, yours included; the day is billed nothing. If STK signs again that day, it is with the day's other key, or a new one when both are invalid, and rows signed with that one check. The rows you keep are not touched, and no other day is: each day has its own keys.

Who acts. A person at STK: finding out that a key leaked, and running the one command that marks it invalid. Nothing finds a leak by itself.

Your own key leaks

What happens. Anyone with your live key can get permissions signed and billed to your account until the key stops, and can make new keys or change the account's bank account, which can lock you out. POST /v1/keys/revoke with your live key stops every key of the account at once (how).

Who pays. Nothing signed after the revoke is billed. What was signed before it is billed as usual: STK cannot tell which of it was not yours.

What you can do. Revoke as soon as you know, then take new keys with your account's bank account. Each step is in GET /v1/ledger.

New keys asked with another bank account

What happens. Someone who knows your account's address and has no key can ask for new keys with a bank account that is not the account's. They start 7 days later, and your keys stop then. Until then every answer to your keys carries "notice", and from 2026-12-05 12:00 UTC one email goes to the account's address; before that day no email is sent. A company that reads neither for 7 days loses its keys to that bank account.

What you can do. Act on "notice": one call to POST /v1/keys/cancel with your live key stops it. With your account's own bank account, POST /v1/keys/reissue gives you new keys at once, whatever is waiting.

STK does not see the intent

What happens. STK receives the seal and its time only, so it does not check that the intent and trigger your code put in the row are what the conversation was about, or that your code applied the rules before calling. A call with a seal is signed and billed whatever the row says. stk_verify.py later shows a row the rules did not permit as not a permission; STK cannot know of it.

What STK's keys can and cannot do

What happens. STK can make a receipt with its own key for any seal it chooses. It cannot change a row you hold: the row is sealed, and your copy is yours. It cannot make a row it has never seen pass the checks: a receipt is only for a seal, and only your random bytes and row open it.

A key made on the day it signs

What happens. STK makes its signing keys 200 days ahead, two a day, and keeps them in Stripe, so a server that starts again signs with the same keys. Only when both of a day's keys have been made invalid does STK make a key that day and sign with it at once. Until the next copy, that key's public key is in Stripe, and in the files of the companies that got a receipt signed with it (stk.py and stk.mjs save it), only. If it were deleted in Stripe in that time, what was signed with it passes check 2 only with the company's own saved key.

US bank account required

What happens. STK is paid one way only: a US bank account, debited by STK (ACH). A company with no US bank account cannot have a live key. There is no card, no invoice to pay yourself, and nothing per country.

What you can do. Register a US bank account once (how). The test key needs none.

A debit is refused

What happens. Your bank answers a debit within 4 business days; STK signs from when the debit begins. Refused for want of money, it is debited again 3 days later, 3 tries in all, and STK signs throughout; after the third, STK does not try again, that month is not owed, and nothing stops. Refused for good (a closed account, no such account, or debits not authorized): STK signs nothing for your account from the next call, and the month's email says so. STK cannot debit a refused account again by itself: the US bank network's rules allow a refused debit to be presented again twice at most, and after the other refusals Stripe ends the authorization.

What you can do. Register a bank account (POST /v1/keys/bank with your live key): it is debited at once and STK signs again from your next call. GET /v1/health with your key says whether STK signs for your account.

One email a month

What happens. STK sends one email a month to the account's address, once the month's debits have gone through or one is refused for good (from 2026-12-11 12:00 UTC). Stripe holds no address of the account and sends none, except the one email the US bank network's rules require when the authorization is given. No email comes when a debit begins, when it is tried again, or when it is refused for want of money.

What you can do. GET /v1/health with your key says whether STK signs for your account; GET /v1/ledger gives each day's counts.

One payment is at most $999,999.99

What happens. Stripe takes one debit in US dollars of at most $999,999.99, and does not issue an invoice above it that a bank account can pay. A month above it is debited by STK on its own as several debits of equal amounts (each at most $999,999.98), together its amount to the cent. You see one amount, in the month's one email; no one is asked.

Your bank's daily limit

What happens. If your bank has a daily limit on debits, the several debits of one month can be refused. Stripe gives no answer that tells this refusal from another, so it is handled as your bank's answer says: for want of money, tried again; debits not authorized, refused for good (above).

What you can do. If your month can pass $999,999.99, ask your bank to allow STK's debits on the 1st of the month above its daily limit.

STK's own limit at Stripe

What happens. Stripe limits what STK may debit by bank account over a rolling week, and does not publish the amount. A debit above it is not sent to your bank: STK begins it again the next day, every day until Stripe takes it. It is not one of the 3 tries, and STK signs for your account throughout.

A test key in production, a live key in a test

What happens. STK cannot tell where a key is used. A test key in production gets the same answers, is never billed, and its rows check as a test key's, not as live permissions. A live key in a test is billed.

Whether an address has keys can be learned

What happens. POST /v1/keys with an address that has keys answers 409. Anyone can learn that way, 3 addresses an hour from one place, whether an address has keys at STK. Not the account, its keys or its use.

What you can do. Get your keys with an address that does not name your company.

The site's keys.json is late

What happens. /keys.json is as of the site's last deploy; a key made after it is in GET /v1/rules?keys=1 only.

What you can do. Save GET /v1/rules?keys=1 yourself.

What Render keeps

What happens. Render runs STK's server, so every request passes through it, with the address it comes from. What Render keeps of a request, and for how long, is under Render's own terms; STK has not read it.

Where the public keys are

What happens. Every public key STK signs with is written to Stripe before it signs anything. It is copied, never shortened and never changed, into this site's public repository by a job GitHub is asked to run every hour (from 2026-11-17 to 2026-11-26 it ran 26 times, 4 to 12 hours apart), and published at /keys.json when the site is next deployed. The copy is a second place to check rows with; STK never takes keys from it. When STK starts, it compares the copy with Stripe: a key deleted or changed in Stripe is then missing or different in GET /v1/rules?keys=1, /v1/health says "ok": false, and the copy still has it as it was published. A key STK makes on the day it signs, when both of the day's keys were made invalid, signs that day: until the next copy, it is in Stripe and in the files of the companies it signed for only.

A day STK started again on

What happens. STK writes each company's count of the day to Stripe every 2 minutes. When its server starts again in a day (a deploy, or after it stopped), it cannot know which seals were counted before the start, so it never adds the two counts: the day is billed as the larger of them, less every person joining that day. The permissions of the smaller count, and those not yet written when the server stopped, are not billed. A person told to STK with a seal in the first 10 minutes of a day, by a server that started after the day before began, is taken off both days. A bill can come out lower than the number of actions STK permitted. It cannot come out higher: on a day the API restarted, the day is billed the larger of the counts before and after the restart, not their sum, so a seal sent again after the restart is not billed twice. STK's own libraries do not send the same seal twice. STK deploys at any time of day, each deploy in the Changelog; the later in a day it deploys, the more of that day's count can be left unbilled. While a deploy starts the server, a grant from STK's last answer lets stk.py and stk.mjs finish and queue the seals. A deploy is not counted as a stop. A start of the same commit again (a crash, a kill, a restart) is counted as an unexpected stop: more than 1 in 250 days makes /v1/health say ok false for 24 hours.

Who pays. STK.

An address that is not yours

What happens. STK does not check that whoever signs up owns the address: it sends no email when keys are made. Someone could take keys under an address that is not theirs; a live key needs a bank account registered, and what they ask is debited from that bank account, but the month's email goes to the address. New keys for an account come only from its live key; from the account's own bank account, proven on a bank page (how); from another bank account, 7 days after they are asked, unless the live key cancels them; or, while its keys have never been used, from the same address again, 10 minutes after they were made. The keys before stop each time.

STK that cannot reach Stripe

What happens. STK signs only with keys whose public keys it has already written to Stripe; each day's keys are made 200 days ahead. With no published key for the day it signs nothing: every ask with a key is answered escalate, with no row, and nothing is billed. If it cannot read whether an account is unpaid for two hours, it answers 503: stk.py and stk.mjs finish under a grant they hold and send the seal when STK answers again; with no grant, escalate. Your conversations go to people, as your code does today. GET /v1/health says "ok": false. When it can write a public key again, the next ask is answered by the rules. Nobody has to do anything. The same 503 comes for a moment when STK is deployed, until the process before it stops.

A person who joins on a later day

What happens. A call to POST /v1/joined takes a permission of the same day (UTC) off the bill; with the seal of a permission of the day before, until 00:10 UTC, one of that day. A person told to STK after that takes nothing off the day before: its permission stays billed.

The same conversation on two servers

What happens. stk.py and stk.mjs do not call STK again for a conversation and intent that got finish the same day, and after a restart they read that day's file again. Each server remembers only its own. If the same conversation is asked on two servers, each calls STK, and each signed seal is a permission: two. With a function of yours in place of the file (ON_ROW, setOnRow), they remember only while the process runs.

A function stopped as soon as it answers

What happens. stk.py and stk.mjs keep a permission's line before ask() returns; one that cannot be kept is told to STK as a person joining, and ask() returns escalate. When STK's answer comes after 3 seconds and no grant holds, the file tells STK a person has the conversation, after ask() has returned. Where your code runs as a function that is stopped as soon as it answers (a serverless function), it may be stopped before that: the permission stays billed.

Who pays. You.

What you can do. Let the function run on for a few seconds after ask() returns, so the file can tell STK. Do not call stk.joined() for every escalate: a conversation that got no finish would take another permission off the day.

A seal waiting in the queue is sent only while the process runs: in such a function, by a later call of the same instance, or before it returns by stk.flush() (await flush() in stk.mjs), which waits up to 30 seconds.

A permission finished before STK signed it

What happens. While the grant of STK's last answer holds (1 hour at most) and STK cannot be reached, stk.py and stk.mjs return finish and keep the seal in a queue. Until STK signs it, there is a queued line and no receipt. If STK then does not sign it (a debit refused for good, a key replaced, the limits of a day, a grant STK refuses), the file writes a not_signed line: the action finished, and there is no permission for it. A queue kept only through ON_ROW, with no files, is lost when the process ends; with files, a restarted process sends the queued lines of today and yesterday. A receipt from the queue is signed under the version of the rules the grant named, and billed for the day STK signed it, not the day recorded. STK's clock is in the receipt; the recorded time is the company's own claim, and STK checks only that it falls within the grant.

Who pays. A seal STK signs is billed as any permission. One it does not sign is billed nothing; the action it was for has no permission.

One file on a network disk

What happens. stk.py and stk.mjs add each line to the day's file in one write. On a disk of one machine, lines of several servers sharing the file never mix. On a network disk (NFS and the like) that is not assured.

What you can do. Give each server its own folder (STK_ROWS_DIR).

How much STK signs

What happens. A live key has no limit. A test key: 1,000 permissions a day (UTC). The seals of a day, of all accounts together, are each counted once, up to 129,138,682 in a day (129,138,682 seals in 1408 MB, filled once on 2026-11-25 on a Mac; the next one was refused); past that, STK signs no new seal that day and the file answers escalate. They are kept in the API's memory only: when the API restarts they are lost, and a seal signed before the restart and sent again the same day is counted again in the count after the restart; the day is billed the larger of the two counts, not their sum, so it is not billed twice. Past these, STK signs nothing more that day and the file answers escalate, with the reason in not_signed. 300 new accounts an hour are made in all, and 3 calls an hour come from one address; then POST /v1/keys answers 429, saying when it opens again (a call with one of your keys is not held up). One account can make 10 reports in 24 hours.

Who pays. Nobody: what is not signed is not billed.

What a load test found

What happens. A record of a load test run from 2026-11-19: of 150 companies, 450 company-days, 300 days were billed less than the test counted, by 1.32% in all. No day was billed more. The cause, found on 2026-11-25: on a day STK's server starts again, each process counts its own share of a company's day, and a process that started cannot know which seals the one before it counted; so the day is billed by the largest share, less every share's people joining, and the smaller shares are not billed. It never bills twice; it bills less. Those were the days the server started again. STK's server starts again when new code is deployed (at any time, each in the Changelog), or when Render, which runs it, starts it again. It is kept as it is: anything else would keep a record that grows with every permission, or could bill twice. A second run of the same 150 companies, started 2026-11-21 at 04:36 UTC, was stopped at 14:15 UTC the same day, before it ended.

Who pays. Nobody is billed more: on those days the bill was lower than the test's own count.

Keys never used

What happens. An account with no day of POST /v1/ask or POST /v1/joined sent to Stripe in the 30 days after it was made is deleted, with its email address. A day lost when STK's server stopped does not count. The same address can get keys again.

What you can do. Make one ask() that gets finish with your live key within 30 days (one with the test key does not count).

The return from the bank page lost. The live key is given when the bank page's session comes back. If the page is closed, or the tab with the keys, before it comes back, POST /v1/keys with the address and the test key gives the live key if the bank account was registered, or a new bank page if not.

What a receipt does not prove

What happens. A receipt does not prove that every conversation was sealed (a company can choose not to call STK); that the row is a true record of the conversation; that a person actually took over, or what that person did; that the time is attested by anyone other than STK; that STK's handling of its signing keys has been audited (it has not); or that the rules are sufficient for any one company's legal duties. What a receipt proves, and what it does not

Who answers for it. Each company, for its own records and its own legal duties.

No real bill yet

What happens. No customer has been billed. One real invoice was raised, sent by Stripe to STK's own test company on 2026-11-17, and voided.

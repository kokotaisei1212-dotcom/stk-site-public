Price

Price

The rules are free. The price is for a receipt. The rules are published under CC BY 4.0, and using them costs nothing, with STK or without it (the licence). What is priced is the receipt: an optional proof of conformity by a third party, STK, signed for one action. The receipt is not part of the rules, and following the rules does not need one.

  • Price is 35% of the cost of the same work by a person, published or derived; where there is no figure, $5. The table.
  • By the key's use: $7, $5 or $3 for each permission (the table; a key with no use, $5) under these rules (the domain conversation, what it covers): each seal your live key sends that STK signs.
  • $0 when a person joins it the same day (UTC). Told by 00:10 UTC the day after, with the permission's seal (stk.py and stk.mjs send it while the process that got the permission still runs), it still counts for its day. Told on a later day after that: it stays billed.
  • Each Monday, Stripe makes the invoice for the week before (Monday to Sunday, UTC) and charges it the same day to the card you registered when you took your live key. STK has no screen for it and sends no email about it. It is due when issued.

Not billed

  • Anything with your test key.
  • Every ask the rules answer escalate: the file does not call STK.
  • The same conversation and intent asked again the same day (UTC) on the same server: the file does not call STK again.
  • A seal STK did not sign: unpaid 60 days, no published key, or STK not answering.
  • An answer that came after your code stopped waiting, or never reached it, while no grant held: the file tells STK a person has it. Under a grant the file finishes, and the permission is billed. If STK cannot be told then: an answer that came late is told as soon as STK answers (after 00:10 UTC the day after, it stays billed); an answer that never reached your code is told once, and if STK cannot be told then, it stays billed.
  • On a day (UTC) STK's server started again (a deploy, or after it stopped): the permissions of the smaller of the two counts, before and after the start. The day is billed as the larger count less every person joining that day. Each count is written to Stripe every 2 minutes; what was not written when the server stopped is not billed.
  • A day whose signing key STK made invalid, for every company. Not yet on an invoice: taken off. Already on one: a credit note from Stripe (an open invoice owes less; a paid one is refunded, or credited to the next invoice when it cannot be).

If an invoice is not paid

  1. If the card is declined, the invoice stays open: Stripe tries the card again, and the invoice can be paid from its link. STK asks Stripe to send an open invoice again on its due date and 7, 30 and 53 days after. GET /v1/health with your key lists your unpaid invoices.
  2. Sixty days after it was issued and still unpaid, STK signs nothing for your account: the file answers escalate to every ask, and nothing is billed.
  3. When it is paid, STK signs again.

How the price is worked out

Price is 35% of the cost of the same work by a person, published or derived, rounded to the dollar. Where no figure has been found, the price is $5.

What STK charges for

STK charges for one thing: a permission it signed. Nothing else.

STK does not decide what a company routes through it. Every permission signed with a key is billed at the price of the key's use, whatever the action.

The price of each use

When you take keys you choose their use, one for each key. Every permission signed with a key is billed at its use's price, whatever the action: STK never learns the action. One account holds keys of several uses, on one card and one invoice: POST /v1/keys with your live key and a use gives keys of another use (Docs). A key taken before uses, or with none chosen, is of the use Everything else, $5, as before.

Why $7 for health plans, not $14. The $40 of Sahni et al. (Health Affairs Scholar 1(5):qxad053, 2023) is the whole administrative cost of one claim, not the cost of the decision. The $20.24 is the cost of the decision itself, derived from a reviewer's wage and the decisions a reviewer makes in a year: BLS OEWS, May 2025, registered nurses' median annual wage, $97,550, × 1.4265 (BLS ECEC, December 2025, total compensation to wages) ÷ 6,875 decisions a year (27.5 a day × 250 working days; the 27.5 a day is the claim of a vendor, Toney Healthcare, not a published study, and has not been verified). The 35% is taken of the latter: $7.08, $7.

STK does not verify the use. It is your declaration and your responsibility; running actions under a key of another use breaks the Terms. The rows that would show it stay with you, and STK never sees them.

Why a card, not an invoice

  • STK has no collections function. No dunning, no legal, no negotiation. One dunning action costs more than a $5 charge is worth, so STK does not do it.
  • A card is charged weekly, on the Monday after the week closes. The customer does nothing after the first minute.
  • STK never receives a bank account number or a card number. The card is held by Stripe.

Why an invoice the company pays itself would not work. An invoice needs someone to chase it; STK has no collections function, one chase costs more than the charge, and a card removes the need.

Why Stripe

  • STK holds no billing ledger and has no store of its own. Every count is written to Stripe, where the customer can read it without asking STK.
  • The record is Stripe's, so it survives STK disappearing, and STK cannot quietly revise its own numbers.
  • The invoice and the payment page are Stripe's. STK has no billing screen of its own.

Counting. Every billed day appears on the company's Stripe invoice as its own entry, with that day's count of permissions. The company reads the count on its own invoice, without asking STK. STK holds no billing ledger of its own; once an invoice is issued, its lines are fixed, and a correction can only be a credit note the company sees. If a count is wrong, it is wrong in a place the company can see.

Why weekly. A week (Monday to Sunday, UTC) is closed the following Monday and charged that day. This is STK's own condition, not a requirement on the company. Its effect for the company: the amount is small and frequent rather than large and monthly, the first charge arrives within days of the first use, and stopping costs nothing: there is no term and no notice period.

What STK holds

  • One right only: if an account is unpaid for 60 days, STK stops signing. It is a condition in the Terms, not part of the rules. There is no other remedy and no interest.

Currency and tax

US dollars. The invoice is, for each key's use, its price times the week's count of permissions in conversations. STK adds no tax to it.
